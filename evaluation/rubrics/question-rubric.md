# Rating rubric: is this question grounded in the passage?

**For:** Sinhala subject teachers rating questions for RQ1.

You will see a passage from a textbook, a question, its options, and the option marked as the
answer. You are **not** told how the question was made. Rate each question on its own, in the
`rating` column, with exactly one of these words:

| Rating | Use it when |
| --- | --- |
| `grounded` | The passage alone shows the marked answer is right, **and** no other option is also right according to the passage. |
| `ungrounded` | The marked answer is wrong, **or** the passage does not say it, **or** another option is also right, **or** the question asks about something the passage does not contain. |
| `unclear` | You cannot decide from the passage: for example, the Sinhala is ambiguous, or the question is too badly worded to judge. |

## Rules

- Judge **from the passage only**, not from what you know about the subject. A true fact the
  passage does not state is `ungrounded`.
- A clumsy but correct question is still `grounded`. Wording quality is not what this rates.
- A question that gives its own answer away is `ungrounded`.
- Do not discuss items with the other raters until everyone has finished.
- If you use `unclear` often, tell the researcher: it may mean the rubric needs work.

## Worked examples

| Passage says | Question and marked answer | Rating |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "ශ්‍රී ලංකාවේ අගනුවර ශ්‍රී ජයවර්ධනපුර කෝට්ටේ වේ." | "ශ්‍රී ලංකාවේ අගනුවර කුමක්ද?" → ශ්‍රී ජයවර්ධනපුර කෝට්ටේ | `grounded` |
| The same passage | "ශ්‍රී ලංකාවේ විශාලතම නගරය කුමක්ද?" → කොළඹ | `ungrounded` (the passage does not say) |
| The same passage | Two options both say කෝට්ටේ, in different spellings | `ungrounded` (two options are right) |
