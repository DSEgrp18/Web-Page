# Information sheet (draft)

**Study:** Design and evaluation of an accessible Sinhala document reader with integrated
text-to-speech and question answering.
**Researchers:** [names, institution, contact], to be completed.

## What is this study about?

We have built Swara, a website that reads Sinhala textbooks aloud and lets a student ask
questions about them, practise with questions, and see what to revise. We want to find out
whether blind and low-vision students can use it on their own, and where it gets in the way.

We are testing **the website, not you.** There are no right or wrong answers, and nothing you
do affects your marks, your school, or your teachers' view of you.

## What would I do?

- One session of about 45–60 minutes, at a place and time that suits you.
- You would use your own screen reader (NVDA or TalkBack) and, if you like, your own phone or
  computer.
- We would ask you to do a few things with the website, such as finding a chapter and listening,
  asking a question, and answering practice questions, and to say what you are thinking as you go.
- Afterwards, two short questions about how easy the website was.

## What is recorded?

- Whether each task was finished, how long it took, and whether you asked for help.
- Notes of what you say while using it.
- An audio recording **only if you agree**. You can say no, and still take part.

We do **not** record your name with any of this; we use a code instead. Nothing you do in the
website during the session is kept after the study. Only totals, never anything about one person,
appear in the research.

## Do I have to take part?

No. You can say no, or stop at any time, even in the middle of a task, without giving a reason.
If you stop, you can ask for what was recorded about you to be deleted.

## Are there risks?

Very few. Some tasks may be frustrating if the website does not work well; that is useful for
us to know, and you can skip any task. Take a break whenever you want.
